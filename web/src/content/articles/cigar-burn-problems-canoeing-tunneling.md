Every cigar smoker eventually sits down with a beautiful stick that decides to burn like a campfire in a crosswind. One side races ahead, a tunnel bores through the center, or the burn line looks like a seismograph reading. These are among the most frustrating moments in the hobby, and also among the most fixable. Understanding *why* a cigar burns badly is the first step to saving the smoke.

## Why is my cigar burning unevenly in the first place?

There are five main reasons why a cigar burns unevenly: the cigar was not lit correctly, the cigar is canoeing, the cigar is tunneling, the cigar is over-humidified, or the cigar is underfilled. Some of those causes trace back to your storage and technique, both fully controllable. Others are construction defects baked in at the factory, and those are harder to overcome.

It's not uncommon for a premium cigar to show an uneven burn for a minute or two at the start, but in most cases a well-made cigar will self-correct as you smoke it. So before reaching for the lighter, give it a puff or two. If it keeps diverging after the first third, that's when intervention is warranted.

Tight draws, tunneling, and canoeing almost always come from some mix of three things: construction, humidification, and technique.

---

## What exactly is canoeing, and why does it happen?

*A question we hear often:*

The most common burn issue is cigar canoeing, where your stogie only burns down one side. This uneven burning pattern resembles the shape of a canoe, hence the name.

A humidity imbalance, lighting your cigar incorrectly, and uneven wrapper thickness can all cause your smoke to burn predominantly down one side. There's also a mechanical cause: the cigar was rolled incorrectly or is under-filled. When you draw on an under-filled cigar, oxygen and air pass through the loosest part faster than the rest, which accelerates the burn in one area.

Humidor habits play a bigger role than most collectors realize. Overloading your humidor can restrict airflow, which causes some cigars to hold an uneven amount of moisture, and that's often what carves out a canoe. If you notice that certain cigars in your collection consistently canoe while others don't, check whether they were sitting near a poorly distributed humidification source.

## How do I fix a canoeing cigar mid-smoke?

*This one comes up a lot:*

The standard first move is rotation. Rotating your cigar periodically between your fingers while smoking will usually prevent canoeing, since it promotes even airflow and lets the ash heat more evenly. Keeping proper humidity levels in your humidor also helps counteract significant moisture differences in the first place.

If rotation alone doesn't close the gap within a minute or two, go for a touch-up. Toast the side that's burning more slowly, but be careful not to speed up the burn so much that you overcorrect. Use the outermost edge of a torch flame, or better yet a soft-flame lighter, and hold it an inch away from the lagging part of the foot rather than directly on it.

If the situation is severe, stop smoking, let the cigar burn out, cut the entire foot off from just behind the ash, and relight. If that doesn't work, the cigar was not assembled correctly and may not be worth preserving.

## What is tunneling, and how is it different from canoeing?

If a cigar tunnels, its filler tobaccos burn faster than the binder and wrapper leaf, leaving the center hollowed out while you're still smoking. Unlike canoeing, which is a *side-to-side* problem, tunneling is a *center-versus-outside* problem.

When you puff on a cigar that's tunneling, the flavor will be off because the tobaccos are burning out of sync. This interferes greatly with a cigar's balance.

A contrast in moisture levels between the inside and outside of your cigar is often the cause, specifically when the interior is drier than the outer layers. Overly slow smoking can also lead to tunneling: if you aren't drawing at a consistent pace, the burning end cools and only the core (the hottest part of the cigar) stays lit, so the inner filler keeps smoldering and bores a hole straight through the center.

The telltale sign of tunneling is an immediate drop in smoke volume and density. If your cigar suddenly seems to stop producing smoke without going out, look at the foot. You'll likely see a hollow core.

To fix it: light the outside tobacco with a lighter or match and take regular, consistent puffs to maintain an even burn throughout the length of the cigar. Another option is letting the cigar cool and go out completely, then cutting below the tunneling burn and relighting. The downside is that you may need to make several cuts and lose a good chunk of the stick.

## How does lighting technique cause burn problems?

*Readers frequently ask:*

The most common reason a cigar burns unevenly, especially in the first third, is a rushed or uneven light. When one side of the foot gets more heat than the other during the toast, combustion starts lopsided from the first draw. The better-lit side burns ahead; the other side smolders and lags. From that point, the cigar burns unevenly throughout unless you actively correct it.

Cutting matters more than people like to admit. A lazy, shallow cut that only opens a tiny slit will make even a well-made cigar feel tight and force most of the airflow through one edge of the bunch. A wildly deep cut on a fragile cap can cause unraveling and strange airflow channels. Always check your cold draw before lighting. If the draw is already stiff or heavily restricted before fire enters the picture, no amount of careful toasting will fully compensate.

When toasting, rotate the foot slowly *above* the flame, not inside it, so heat applies evenly across the entire circumference before you take your first puff. This single habit eliminates a large percentage of early-burn problems.

| Burn Problem | What It Looks Like | Primary Cause | Best Fix |

| Canoeing | One side burns faster, creating a canoe channel | Uneven light, humidity imbalance, under-filled spot | Rotate cigar; touch up lagging side with soft flame |
| Tunneling | Center hollow, wrapper intact | Smoking too slowly, dry interior, bad foot light | Consistent puffs; light the outer wrapper; cut and relight |
| Runner / Crack burn | A burn line races along the wrapper seam | Wrapper seam dried out, construction defect | Lick or dampen the racing line; touch up surrounding area |

| Wavy burn line | Irregular but not fully canoeing | Slight humidity variation, minor soft spot | Usually self-corrects; rotate if not resolved in 2-3 draws |

## Does my humidor storage actually affect how a cigar burns?

Yes, significantly. Over-humidification is one of the most common and least-diagnosed culprits. Over-humidified cigars burn like wet firewood. Let your humidor swing up into the seventies or pack it too tight, and tunneling and canoeing show up much more often.

While the same issues that cause uneven burn can cause canoeing, it's most commonly a symptom of humidification problems, namely not rotating cigars in your humidor often enough. The fix is straightforward: keep your cigar's environment as stable as possible, rotate your cigars from bottom to top occasionally, and open your humidor for about 15 minutes once a week to let in some fresh room air.

For collectors storing large quantities, poor airflow near the humidification source creates micro-zones of uneven moisture. Cigars resting directly against a foam or gel humidifier can absorb moisture on one side only, and those are exactly the cigars that canoe. If you track your cigars and their humidor position in a catalog app like [Stick Picks](https://stickpicks.app), you can spot which shelf positions or humidifier-adjacent spots are producing problematic burns and adjust your rotation accordingly.

## Is it ever just a bad cigar?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Sometimes, yes. The flavor changes for the worse, and sometimes all the touch-ups in the world won't fix the problem. Under-filled cigars are rare in the premium handmade segment, but they do exist, especially in budget lines or poorly quality-controlled batches. If you've rotated, touched up, adjusted your pace, and recut twice and the burn still won't behave, the construction is the issue, not your technique.

Worth noting: even top-rated premium cigars occasionally have a bad stick. Industry-wide quality control data consistently shows that even highly rated blends carry a small defect rate. Some sources in the trade estimate 2-5% of any production run has a construction soft spot that will manifest as a burn problem. That's simply the nature of a hand-rolled, agricultural product.

When a cigar is genuinely unfixable, the best move is to set it down and pull out a different stick. Life is too short for a smoke that fights you the whole way.

## 5 habits that prevent cigar burn problems before they start

1. Toast the foot evenly. Rotate the cigar slowly above, not inside, the flame until the full circumference glows before the first draw.
2. Pace yourself. One draw roughly every 45-60 seconds is the sweet spot for most ring gauges. Faster puffs overheat the filler; slower ones let the wrapper cool and separate.
3. Store at stable humidity. Target 65-70% RH and check your hygrometer at least weekly.
4. Rotate your stock. Uneven humidity within the cigar can cause one side to burn faster than the other, and this happens most often when cigars sit in the same position for weeks without being moved.
5. Check the cold draw first. If it's already difficult before you light, you're starting with either a construction problem or a bad cut, and you can fix that before fire complicates things.

## FAQs

What is cigar canoeing?
Canoeing is when one side of a cigar burns significantly faster than the other, hollowing out a channel that resembles the shape of a canoe. It's the most common cigar burn problem and is usually caused by an uneven light or uneven moisture distribution inside the cigar.

What causes a cigar to tunnel?
Tunneling happens when the filler tobacco in the center burns faster than the wrapper and binder, leaving the outer leaf intact while the inside becomes hollow. Smoking too slowly, uneven lighting of the foot, and moisture imbalance between the interior and exterior of the cigar are the primary causes.

How do I fix a canoeing cigar?
Rotate the cigar in your fingers to promote even airflow. If rotation doesn't self-correct it within a minute or two, use a soft flame lighter to gently touch up the slower-burning side. In persistent cases, let the cigar go out, knock off the ash, and relight from a fresh cut just behind the burn line.

Can over-humidification cause burn problems?
Yes. Over-humidified cigars burn like wet wood, slowly, unevenly, and with compressed flavor. Keeping your humidor at a stable 65-70% RH and allowing freshly acquired cigars to rest for at least two weeks before smoking dramatically reduces humidity-related burn issues.

Is it the cigar's fault or mine when it burns badly?
Both are possible. Technique issues, a rushed light, puffing too fast, or smoking in the wind, account for the majority of burn problems. Construction defects like an underfilled spot or a rolled-in stem can also cause persistent burns that no amount of touch-ups will fully fix.
