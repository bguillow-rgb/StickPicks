## What is the ideal temperature for cigars?

[ 200-300 words. The 65-70°F range. Why 68°F is a common setpoint.
What changes at each end: warmer = faster aging + beetle risk;
cooler = slower aging + reduced wrapper oil mobility. Cite a primary
source, Boveda, Drew Estate, or *Cigar Aficionado*, on the range. ]

## What happens above 74°F?

[ 250-350 words. Tobacco beetle (Lasioderma serricorne) life cycle:
eggs are dormant in nearly all cigars at packaging time; sustained
74°F+ for 48-72h triggers hatch; larvae bore through wrappers in
clear circular pinholes (1-2mm). Damage: a humidor can be reduced
to scrap in days. Identification: fine dust, perfect circular
pinholes, larvae visible under bands.

First-hand: have you had a beetle event? What temperature spike
caused it? ]

## What happens below 60°F?

[ 200-300 words. Aging effectively halts. Wrapper oils become
sluggish, which dulls flavor. Long-term sub-60°F storage is fine for
preservation but not aging. Cuban-style aging is generally done in
the 65-68°F band specifically because the slower kinetics produce
the desired chemistry. ]

## Does temperature affect humidity?

[ 250-350 words. The physics: relative humidity is a function of
temperature and absolute moisture. Heat the same humidor and RH
drops; cool it and RH rises. Rule of thumb: every 10°F shift moves
RH 4-6 percentage points. The summer trap: hygrometer reads 68% but
the humidor is at 78°F, humidity is fine, temperature is in beetle
range. Always check both, not just RH. ]

## How do you keep a humidor at temperature year-round?

[ 250-350 words. Placement strategy: interior closet, ground floor,
away from exterior walls and HVAC vents. Insulation: cabinet humidors
buffer better than desktop. Active solutions: thermoelectric coolers,
wineadors with temperature control, dedicated cigar coolers (Newair
and similar). Cost vs collection size, when does it become worth it.

First-hand: where do you keep yours, what swings have you seen? ]

## What about freezing cigars to kill beetles?

[ 200-300 words. The freeze-purge protocol:
1. Seal cigars in a freezer bag with a 65% Boveda pack.
2. Refrigerator 24 hours.
3. Freezer (0°F or below) for 72 hours.
4. Refrigerator 24 hours.
5. Humidor 72 hours before smoking.

Why the gradual step-down: rapid temperature change cracks wrappers
and shocks the leaf. When to do it: any new arrival from a less
trusted source, any humidor with confirmed beetle activity, any
collection moved from a hot environment. ]
