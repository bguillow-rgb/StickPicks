Cigar bundles are one of the best values in the hobby, often the same quality leaf as a boxed line sold without the packaging markup. But the moment you bring one home, a real question emerges: now what? Unlike a factory box with its cedar walls and tight-fitting lid, a bundle arrives wrapped in plastic and offers zero humidity protection of its own. Getting the storage right from day one makes all the difference.

## Wait, what exactly is a cigar bundle, and how is it different from a box?

*A question we hear often:*

A cigar bundle is simply a group of cigars, typically 20, 25, or sometimes 50 sticks, bound together and wrapped in plastic shrink-wrap or brown paper with no cedar box. The 25-count format is the most common at retail.

Bundles are generally reserved for factory seconds and overruns, essentially the same cigars as the boxed versions but with minor imperfections, sold at a considerably lower price. That said, a growing number of manufacturers deliberately bundle first-quality blends to pass the savings from skipping box production directly to the buyer. So the format alone tells you nothing definitive about what's inside. You need to know the brand and the line.

The critical difference for storage is cedar. One reason manufacturers put cigars in boxes is to preserve the cedar aging-room environment in which the cigars were kept before shipping. A bundle has none of that built-in buffer, which means the cigars are entirely dependent on your humidor from the moment the plastic comes off.

## Should I remove the plastic wrapping before putting bundles in my humidor?

Yes, and this is the single step most collectors get wrong.

The outer plastic shrink-wrap on a cigar bundle is not porous the way individual-cigar cellophane is. Plastic wrap is not permeable to air or moisture. Leaving the bundle sealed inside your humidor means the humidity you've carefully maintained can't reach the tobacco. The cigars sit in a miniature sealed pocket, neither gaining nor losing moisture in any controlled way. That's a recipe for uneven conditioning and trapped ammonia, especially in freshly rolled sticks.

The right move: cut or peel the plastic off completely, then store the cigars loose in your humidor. To keep cigars from different bundles from mixing, group them in a cedar-lined tray, use dividers, or tuck them into an empty cigar box from a previous purchase. Keep bundles separated from each other to allow air circulation and prevent sticking.

One exception worth knowing: if a bundle arrived sealed and you don't plan to touch it for six months or more, some collectors leave it in its original plastic in a coolidor just to minimize disturbance. But the moment you open the bundle to start smoking from it, remove all the plastic from the remaining sticks.

## What are the ideal humidity and temperature settings for bundle cigars?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Bundle cigars follow the same fundamental storage rules as any premium cigar. The target is 65-72% relative humidity and 65-70°F (18-21°C), moist enough to keep the tobacco burning slowly and evenly, but dry enough to prevent mold.

For collectors storing bundles that may be factory seconds or overruns, leaning toward the lower end of that range, around 65-67% RH, is a smart call. Fresher, greener tobacco benefits from a slightly drier environment that encourages slow ammonia off-gassing rather than trapping moisture that amplifies harsh notes. A humidor that reads 68% but swings between 62% and 74% is more damaging than one that holds a steady 72%, so stability matters more than hitting a precise number.

| Storage Scenario | Recommended RH | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bundle cigars, smoking within 3 months | 65, 68% | Allows steady acclimation |
| Bundle cigars, aging 6, 24 months | 65, 67% | Encourages ammonia dissipation |
| Mixed humidor (bundles + boxed cigars) | 65, 68% | Universal sweet spot |
| Coolidor or tupperdor with bundles | 65, 69% | Airtight seals run slightly higher |
| Budget bundles (seconds/overruns) | 65, 67% | Drier helps purge green harshness |

If you're using a tupperdor or coolidor rather than a traditional humidor, both are solid options for bulk bundle storage. A Boveda 65% or 69% pack is the easiest set-and-forget solution in airtight containers where there's no cedar to buffer moisture swings.

## Do cigar bundles age well, or is it a waste of time?

*Readers frequently ask:*

Honestly, it depends on what's in the bundle. Quality tobacco ages regardless of whether it arrived in cedar or plastic. The packaging is a delivery mechanism, not a determinant of the leaf's potential.

Even before a manufacturer decides to bundle rather than box a cigar, the tobacco has already been cured and aged prior to selection. So a bundle of first-quality cigars from a serious manufacturer has gone through the same pre-roll aging as that maker's boxed line. Get them into proper humidor conditions and the home aging process picks up where the factory left off.

Some bundle cigars, particularly seconds, may not have been aged after rolling. They were bundled immediately and can still carry ammonia or other compounds that make for a harsh early smoke. This is why the 2-3 month rest minimum matters so much for bundles. Rest gets you through early ammonia dissipation. Aging is the longer-term process, more like storing red wine, where flavors mellow and develop complexity over time.

Time doesn't fix everything. A weak cigar won't become a good one just because it sat in a humidor for three years. Budget machine-made bundles are typically best smoked fresh. But a quality handmade bundle from a reputable brand? Give it six months at minimum, and you may be surprised at how much the rough edges smooth out.

## How do I actually organize loose bundle cigars in my humidor?

Without a factory box to anchor them, bundle cigars can quickly become a jumbled mess, especially once you're pulling from several different bundles at once.

A few practical systems that work well:

Cedar tray method: Place bundle cigars in the standard cedar trays your humidor came with, grouped by blend or strength. Label each row with a strip of painter's tape and a marker, cheap, removable, and readable at a glance.

Empty box method: Re-use empty cedar cigar boxes from previous purchases as corrals for loose bundles. Storing cigars in repurposed boxes rather than loose on trays keeps the microenvironment tighter and aging more consistent.

Rubber band grouping: Keep 5- or 10-cigar sets together with a single rubber band and a small paper tag noting the blend name and date added. This works especially well in a coolidor where you might have dozens of loose sticks.

Because bundles don't have barcode-scannable boxes, you can manually log the blend, count, acquisition date, and purchase price, so your collection value and inventory stay accurate even for the un-boxed portion of your humidor. When cigars lack band art or box codes to reference, a date-stamped journal entry is the only reliable way to know when your bundles are ready.

## My bundles are smoking harsh, is the storage wrong, or is it the cigars?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Harshness in freshly acquired bundles is almost always a rest issue, not a storage problem. Freshly made cigars often carry lingering ammonia and need time in the humidor before they're worth smoking.

Before you touch your humidity levels, give bundle cigars at least 60-90 days in the humidor with no intervention. If they taste green and bitter, give them at least three months. If they don't improve at all after that, they were probably no good to begin with.

If harshness persists past the three-month mark, check two things. First, verify your hygrometer is calibrated. A reading that's off by even 4-5% RH can mean your cigars are sitting too wet or too dry. Second, confirm your humidor temperature is staying below 70°F (21°C). Tobacco beetles hatch above 72°F (22°C), and high heat also accelerates chemical reactions in the leaf that produce acrid smoke, the kind that's easy to mistake for a storage problem when it's actually a temperature problem.

## FAQs

**Should I remove the plastic wrap from a cigar bundle before storing it?**
Yes. The plastic shrink-wrap on a bundle is not permeable to moisture the way cellophane is on individual cigars. Leaving the bundle in plastic prevents humidity from reaching the cigars and traps any residual ammonia, especially in freshly rolled bundles. Unwrap the bundle, then store the cigars loose or in a cedar-lined tray in your humidor.

**Do cigar bundles age as well as boxed cigars?**
They can, but the experience differs. Bundle cigars lack the cedar microclimate of a factory box, so aging depends entirely on your humidor environment. Premium bundle cigars, first-quality blends sold without box packaging, age comparably to their boxed counterparts. Factory seconds and overruns in bundle form vary more. Quality tobacco will still develop with time, but a weak blend won't improve dramatically regardless of how long it rests.

**How long should I rest a cigar bundle after buying it?**
A minimum of 2-3 months at 65-70% RH is a solid baseline. Freshly bundled cigars that are factory seconds or overruns may still carry residual ammonia from the rolling process, so 3-6 months of rest is even better. Think of it as the equivalent of the cigar sick period after shipping. The cigars need time to acclimate and shed any harshness before they're at their best.

**Can I store cigar bundles in a coolidor or tupperdor?**
Absolutely. A coolidor or tupperdor works well for bundle storage, often better than a budget desktop humidor because the airtight seal provides more consistent humidity. Place Spanish cedar sheets alongside or between the loose cigars to add cedar influence. Use a calibrated Boveda pack at 65-69% RH and keep the container in a cool, dark location away from temperature swings.

**How do I keep bundle cigars organized if they don't have a box?**
Use cedar trays, dividers, or even the empty cedar boxes from previous purchases to corral loose bundle cigars by brand or blend. You can also group them by rubber band into small 5- or 10-cigar sets and label them with a piece of masking tape. An app like Stick Picks lets you catalog loose cigars without a box, so you always know what you have and when you added it.
