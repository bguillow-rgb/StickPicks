Cigar trading sits at the intersection of two things collectors already love: discovering new sticks and sharing the hobby with people who get it. Whether you're swapping a box-split to sample a line before committing to a full box, sending an unexpected bomb to a forum friend, or working out a careful value-for-value trade of aged rarities, the mechanics and etiquette are worth understanding before your first package goes out the door.

## So what exactly *is* cigar trading, and how is it different from just buying cigars?

Trading means exchanging cigars from your personal collection with another enthusiast -- no retailer, no markup, just hobbyist-to-hobbyist. It's the most common form of sharing among collectors. Usually practiced between friends, but not limited to people you already know, a trade is simple: you part with sticks from your stash in exchange for sticks from a partner's stash, with both sides having something the other actually wants to smoke.

The community has also developed a few related rituals beyond straight trades:

- **The bomb** -- A "bomb" is shipping cigars to a fellow collector entirely unannounced. The surprise package most often contains sticks from the bomber's personal stash, and the best bombs tend to include cigars that are expensive, rare, or genuinely hard to find.
- **The pass** -- A group of collectors each smoke one cigar from a shared selection and mail the remainder to the next person on a predetermined list, adding tasting notes as it travels.
- **The box split** -- Two or more collectors divide a full box among themselves to get a per-stick price cheaper than buying singles, without anyone committing to 20+ of the same cigar.

Each format has its own loose etiquette. Getting them wrong is a fast way to develop a bad reputation in a surprisingly tight-knit community.

## How do I find people to trade with in the first place?

*A question we hear often:* the cigar trading world looks like an inside game until you know where the doors are.

If you're newer to the hobby, start with a reputable trading site or forum. These platforms give both parties a layer of accountability and typically have systems to track trades and verify participants. PuroTrader, Reddit's r/cigars, and several dedicated forum communities all fall into this category.

The main venues worth knowing:

| Platform | Format | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| r/cigars (Reddit) | Trade/bomb flair threads | Beginners, casual swaps |
| CIGAR.com Forums | Newbie Trade program | First-time traders who need a sponsor |
| CigarPass | Forum with verified trader list | Mid-level collectors |
| PuroTrader | Buy/sell/auction marketplace | Brands like Arturo Fuente, Rocky Patel, Montecristo, and Gurkha at secondary-market prices |
| Cigar Keep Stick Exchange | Peer-to-peer, no commission | Cuban cigar specialists |
| In-person herfs | Face-to-face | Highest trust, zero shipping risk |

Attending an in-person herf is one of the best ways to execute a trade -- direct exchange, no tracking numbers, no waiting on a carrier. Herfs are also a good place to absorb trading culture before you try it online. If you're not sure what a herf is, check out our guide to [what a herf is](https://stickpicks.app/articles/what-is-a-herf-cigar-gathering), which covers the basics and how to find one near you.

On online forums, introduce yourself and participate genuinely before posting a trade offer. Hang out, get to know people. If someone is willing to sponsor your first trade, you'll typically be asked to send your end first as the unestablished party. Once they confirm receipt, they'll send theirs -- and after a few successful trades, you'll have a reputation to your name.

## How do I value cigars fairly for a trade?

*This one comes up a lot:* it's the question that generates the most friction between collectors, and getting it right matters more than almost anything else.

Start with current retail. Look up the cigar on two or three major online retailers and average what you find -- prices fluctuate, so one source can mislead you. For standard production cigars, retail is essentially the floor of trade value.

For aged or discontinued sticks, the math shifts. One method used in collector communities adds a flat 5% per year to the current price. A more aggressive approach doubles the current price for the first ten years, then adds 5% per year after that -- though this only applies to genuinely rare cigars. A twenty-year-old example of a widely available brand may still only be worth a few dollars.

Condition matters enormously and doesn't always show up in a formula. Brand, age, and storage history all affect trade value, but dry, damaged, or poorly stored cigars lose ground regardless of how old they are. If you've kept detailed notes on temperature, RH, and how long your cigars have rested, that provenance data genuinely strengthens your position in a negotiation. ## What's the etiquette for making a fair trade?

The cardinal rule: exchange cigars of approximate value. If a trade partner ships hard-to-find premium cigars, sending a bundle of inexpensive filler in return is not a fair trade, regardless of your personal opinion of those sticks. Don't use trading as a way to clear out cigars you don't want to smoke. Trade things your partner will appreciate, stay in the same price range, and repaying someone with a raw deal is a fast ticket to a bad reputation.

A few other unwritten rules:

- **Communicate before you pack.** Tell your partner exactly what you're sending -- brand, vitola, and approximate purchase date -- so there are no surprises on arrival.
- **Respond to bombs.** Don't be surprised if a sender adds extra cigars beyond the agreed quantity, but if they do, it's good form to send something in return. Those extras are a gesture that calls for reciprocation, not a freebie.
- **Ship promptly.** If you've agreed to a trade, get your package out within 48-72 hours. Sitting on someone else's end for two weeks is considered bad form.
- **Grade yourself honestly.** If a cigar in your collection has a small crack or construction flaw, say so. Your trade partner deserves to know.

## How do I pack and ship cigars for a trade without them arriving damaged?

*Readers frequently ask:* this is a step beginners consistently underestimate, and a bad arrival can poison an otherwise solid trading relationship.

A few non-negotiables:

1. **Include humidity control.** At minimum, tuck one 62% or 65% Boveda pack into the package per 25 cigars. Boveda's 2-way humidity packs both add and absorb moisture to hold a precise RH during transit.
2. **Protect from crushing.** Use a rigid outer box (an old cigar box works perfectly), wrap each cigar individually in bubble wrap or thin cedar sheets, and fill void space with packing paper -- never foam peanuts that can press against wrappers.
3. **Choose the right carrier.** In the United States, USPS prohibits most tobacco shipments between private parties. Use UPS or FedEx, declare the contents accurately, and require an adult signature on delivery.
4. **Insure the package.** If you're sending anything worth more than $50, insure it for the full trade value. Carriers lose packages, and "it was just cigars" is not a conversation you want to have.
5. **Ship mid-week.** Packages that miss a Friday delivery can sit in an unclimate-controlled warehouse over a weekend in summer heat or winter cold. Ship Monday through Wednesday when possible.

Temperature during transit deserves particular attention. Cigars exposed to heat above 80°F (27°C) for extended periods risk beetle activity, a problem that can spread through an entire humidor once the package arrives. ## Should I let traded cigars rest before I smoke them?

Yes -- and this is a step even experienced collectors sometimes skip. Cigars that have traveled through temperature swings and handling stress need time to restabilize in your humidor. Our [cigar sick period guide](https://stickpicks.app/articles/cigar-sick-period-after-shipping) goes into the full science, but the short answer is to rest newly arrived sticks for at least two to three weeks at your target humidity before smoking. For a traded aged rarity, a month is even better.

Once they're resting, log them in your collection immediately. When you receive traded cigars, you often have provenance details -- who owned them, how they were stored, roughly how old they are -- that you'll lose if you don't record them right away. ## What makes someone a "good trader" in the community?

The cigar trading community is reputation-based to a degree that surprises outsiders. And reputation here isn't about having the rarest cigars -- it's about reliability, honesty, and a certain basic generosity.

Good traders share three habits. First, they over-communicate: confirming when they've received a package, flagging if a shipment is delayed, describing what they're sending before it ships. Second, they build trust gradually: when trading outside your immediate circle, start with more affordable sticks, confirm that shipping methods and turnaround times meet expectations, and make sure you're receiving fresh, quality product before the stakes get higher. Third, they contribute beyond trades: posting tasting notes, answering beginner questions, being someone a new collector actually wants to know.

Keeping up with current brands, limited releases, and market prices also matters. Knowing what your cigars are actually worth in trade -- not what you paid, but what the market says today -- is a basic competency. A collection you've diligently cataloged, with purchase dates, storage logs, and tasting notes attached to each entry, tells that story automatically. It's one reason serious traders increasingly keep a digital inventory in apps like Stick Picks alongside their humidor.

## FAQs

**What is a "cigar bomb"?**
A cigar bomb is when a collector ships cigars to a fellow enthusiast completely unannounced -- a surprise package from their personal stash. It's a generous act of community goodwill, and the recipient is generally expected to "retaliate" with a bomb of their own.

**How do I know if a cigar is worth what my trade partner says it is?**
Cross-reference the current retail price at major online retailers, check recent sold listings on cigar trading forums, and factor in storage condition and age. For rare or aged cigars, one common method adds roughly 5% per year to the current retail price as an aging premium.

**What should I include when shipping cigars for a trade?**
Pack cigars in a rigid box or padded mailer with at least one 62% RH Boveda pack per 25 cigars, cellophane intact if present, and bubble wrap or cedar shavings as cushioning. Label the outside as "collectible goods" and insure the package for its full value.

**Where can I find people to trade cigars with online?**
Popular options include the r/cigars subreddit (which has a dedicated trade flair), CIGAR.com forums, CigarPass, PuroTrader, and the Cigar Keep Stick Exchange. Herf events, in-person or virtual, are also good venues for face-to-face trades.

**Is it legal to ship cigars as part of a private trade in the US?**
Shipping cigars between private parties is legal, but you must use an adult-signature-required service and comply with your carrier's policies. USPS prohibits mailing most tobacco products; use UPS or FedEx and declare the contents accurately.
