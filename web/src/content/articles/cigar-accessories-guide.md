Every cigar requires exactly three things before you can smoke it: something to open the cap, something to light the foot, and somewhere to keep it until you're ready. Beyond that core, the accessories market sprawls fast, and a lot of it is noise. This guide organizes everything into tiers: what you need on day one, what earns its place after your first few boxes, and what you can safely ignore.

---

## What cigar accessories do I actually *need* to get started?

The honest short answer: three things. A sharp double-guillotine cutter, a butane torch lighter, and humidity control, either a small humidor or a two-way humidity pack. A V-cut is a popular alternative that creates a wedge-shaped draw, though it concentrates the smoke stream and doesn't suit every cigar. A two-way humidity pack holds your target RH automatically without constant refilling.

Add a dedicated cigar ashtray and your day-one kit is complete. A proper cigar ashtray has wide, deep rests that cradle a fat ring gauge and a bowl deep enough to hold a full session's ash without a breeze scattering it. A standard cigarette ashtray simply can't do that.

That's it for the basics. Everything below is an upgrade worth considering as your collection and experience grow.

## Which cutter should I actually buy, there are so many options

*This one comes up a lot:*

The three main cutter styles each have a real use case, but one is the most versatile starting point.

| Cutter Type | Cut Style | Best For | Main Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Double guillotine | Straight across the cap | Most cigars, most sizes | Blades dull over time; buy quality |
| V-cutter (cat's eye) | Deep wedge notch | Concentrates flavor, good on robusto/toro | Can channel tar on a full-body stick |
| Punch | Small circular hole | Clean, low-debris draw | Doesn't work on figurados or pointy caps |
| Cigar scissors | Straight cut | Precise control, elegant | Expensive; overkill for everyday use |

For most collectors, a sharp double-guillotine handles 90% of cigars in the humidor. Keep a small punch in your car or jacket pocket as a no-fuss backup. See our guide to [v-cut vs straight cut vs punch](/cigar-cut-types-v-cut-vs-straight-cut-vs-punch) for a deeper comparison.

## What kind of lighter do I need for cigars?

*A question we hear often:*

A butane torch is the standard for good reason. A cigar needs to be lit gently and evenly, and a torch flame does it best. A single thin flame forces you to rotate the cigar for a long time; a triple jet covers the whole foot at once, which matters most on a thick ring gauge.

Soft-flame butane lighters are a legitimate alternative for indoor smoking in still air, particularly for mild Connecticut-wrapped cigars where a gentler toast preserves delicate flavors. Cedar spills, thin cedar strips you light from a candle flame, are the most traditional method and add zero chemical taste, making them worth trying for aged or high-end smokes.

What to avoid entirely: standard disposable lighters using naphtha (Zippo-style fluid) and electric arc lighters. Both impart noticeable off-flavors when used to toast a cigar foot.

Get the cutter, the lighter, and the storage right before spending money on anything else.

## How important is a hygrometer, do I need one if I use humidity packs?

Two-way humidity packs (Boveda, Integra) do the humidification work for you without a hygrometer. But a calibrated hygrometer still earns its place as a diagnostic tool.

It's useful for checking conditions from time to time. With a good two-way pack it's a convenience rather than a necessity, but if you buy one, check its calibration. Many analog hygrometers ship 5-10 RH points off, which means you're reading false data every time you open the lid. If you're going to own one, get a digital model and run a calibration check before trusting it. Our full guide on [how to calibrate a hygrometer](/how-to-calibrate-hygrometer-humidor) walks through the salt test and calibration kit methods step by step.

For a collection under 25 cigars maintained on two-way packs, a hygrometer is genuinely optional. Once you're managing 100+ sticks across multiple storage vessels, you need one. Humidity swings can affect months of aging, and you want to catch them early.

## What accessories actually make a difference once I have more than a few boxes?

*Readers frequently ask:*

Once you're maintaining a real humidor with 50 or more cigars in rotation, these go from "nice to have" to actively useful.

**Draw tool.** A cigar draw tool (also called a draw poker or PerfecDraw) is a thin, pointed implement that opens a plugged filler channel from the foot end without damaging the wrapper. At $15-$30, it costs less than most single premium cigars. See our [cigar draw tool guide](/cigar-draw-tool-guide) for how to use one without overdoing it.

**Cedar accessories.** Spanish cedar sheets, dividers, and spills all add measurable value to a maturing collection. Cedar helps stabilize humidity, lends a subtle aromatic quality to aging cigars, and provides a natural deterrent against tobacco beetles, which become a real concern in collections stored above 72°F. If you're building out a wineador or coolidor, lining the interior with cedar sheets makes a real difference. Our guide to [cedar sheets for humidors](/cedar-sheets-dividers-humidor) covers sizing and placement.

**Travel case or travel humidor.** A hard, airtight, waterproof travel case with a Boveda pack inside keeps cigars protected on a trip. A soft leather case works for an evening out, but a hard case is the only sensible choice for flights, hiking, or any context where the cigars might take a knock.

**Cigar rest or stand.** A cigar stand holds a lit cigar horizontally, out of the ashtray, between draws, so the tobacco burns evenly and the wrapper stays clean. It also enables hands-free smoking. Small budget, genuine payoff, especially outdoors or at a table with no ashtray groove handy.

## Is there anything on the accessories market I should skip?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Yes, and knowing what to skip is as useful as knowing what to buy.

**Cheap foam humidifiers.** The sponge-based humidifier included with most entry-level humidors over-humidifies unpredictably, dries out without warning, and is a common source of mold problems. Replace it immediately with a two-way humidity pack or a quality gel-bead system.

**Butane-free electric arc lighters.** These produce a hot, focused arc of plasma that chars rather than toasts the foot of the cigar, creating harsh, uneven combustion on the first draw. They look compelling in ads and underperform in practice.

**Uncalibrated analog hygrometers.** An analog hygrometer reading 69% that's actually measuring 61% gives you false confidence while your cigars quietly dry out. If you buy one, calibrate it before you trust it.

**Oversized starter kits.** Many gift-box accessory kits bundle two or three cutters, a lighter, and a small humidifier, most of them mediocre quality. One excellent cutter and one reliable lighter will serve you far better than four middling versions of each.

## How do accessories change as my collection grows?

The accessories you need map almost directly to collection size. Here's a practical reference:

| Collection Size | Essential | Worthwhile Upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| 1-25 cigars | Cutter, torch lighter, Boveda packs, ashtray | Small desktop humidor, hygrometer |
| 25-100 cigars | Sealed desktop humidor, digital hygrometer, travel case | Cedar dividers, draw tool, cigar rest |
| 100-300 cigars | Cabinet humidor or wineador, bead or electronic humidity control | Multiple hygrometers, cedar sheets, rotation system |
| 300+ cigars | Coolidor or walk-in setup, temperature monitoring | Dedicated aging vessel, collection tracking app |

According to data from cigar industry retailers, the average active collector owns between 50 and 150 cigars at any given time, which puts most enthusiasts squarely in the "25-100" tier, where a digital hygrometer and a draw tool are the two most commonly overlooked upgrades.

## 5 FAQs about cigar accessories

**What are the absolute must-have cigar accessories for a beginner?**
Three things cover the basics: a sharp double-guillotine cutter, a butane lighter (single- or triple-jet torch), and humidity control, either a small humidor with a two-way pack like a Boveda, or a tupperdor setup. Add a proper cigar ashtray and you have a complete starter kit.

**Do I need a hygrometer if I use Boveda packs?**
Not strictly, but it's worth having. Two-way humidity packs handle the work automatically, but a calibrated digital hygrometer lets you spot a failing seal, a drifting pack, or a humidity swing before your cigars suffer. Think of it as a check on your system, not the system itself.

**Is a cigar draw tool worth buying?**
Yes. Once you've smoked more than a handful of premium cigars, you'll eventually hit a tight draw. A draw tool can open a plugged filler without ruining the cigar. At $15-$30, it pays for itself the first time it saves a $20 stick.

**What's the difference between a cigar rest and a cigar ashtray?**
An ashtray is for catching ash and providing a groove to rest the cigar. A standalone rest or stand lifts the lit cigar horizontally, clear of any surface, between draws. That helps maintain an even burn and keeps the wrapper clean. A stand doesn't collect ash, so the two work best together rather than as substitutes.

**What cigar accessories are genuinely not worth buying?**
Avoid cheap foam humidifiers that ship with entry-level humidors. They over-humidify and grow mold. Butane-free electric lighters create uneven heat and char the foot. Don't trust analog hygrometers without calibrating them first; many read 5-10 RH points off straight out of the box.
