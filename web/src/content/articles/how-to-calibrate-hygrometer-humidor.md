Your hygrometer is the single most critical instrument in your humidor. Every humidity decision you make, whether to add a Boveda pack, refill a reservoir, or check on a long-term age, flows through that one number on the dial. If that number is wrong, every decision downstream is wrong too. Yet most collectors never verify the accuracy of their hygrometer at all. Here's how to fix that.

## Why does my humidor hygrometer need calibrating in the first place?

*A question we hear often:* brand-new hygrometers seem like they should just work. The uncomfortable truth is they frequently don't. Some hygrometers can be off by as much as 10% RH right out of the box, before they've ever touched a humidor. A device that reads 65% when your humidor is actually sitting at 75% will have you frantically adding moisture when your cigars are already perfectly hydrated, which is exactly how you invite mold and wrapper splits.

Analog (dial-type) hygrometers are particularly prone to drift because they rely on physical coil springs that lose tension over time, with heat cycles, and after drops. Digital sensors are more stable but are not immune to factory variance. Whether your hygrometer shipped last week or has been in your humidor for two years, the only way to trust the reading is to verify it against a known humidity reference point.

The standard reference for cigar hygrometers is 75% RH, a figure nature provides for free. When table salt and water reach a saturated equilibrium in a sealed space, the resulting relative humidity stabilizes at exactly 75%, regardless of room temperature. That is the foundation of both the DIY salt test and commercial calibration kits.

## What's the salt test and how do I do it step by step?

The salt test is the classic, free, at-home method. You'll need: a resealable zip-top bag (or a small airtight container), a bottle cap or shot glass, plain table salt, and a few drops of water.

1. Place a small mound of salt, about a teaspoon, in the bottle cap.
2. Add water drop by drop until the salt is damp throughout but not dissolved into slush. You want a moist clump, not a brine pool.
3. Set the bottle cap and your hygrometer inside the bag side by side without letting the salt touch the sensor.
4. Seal the bag completely. Air exchange will ruin the test.
5. Leave it undisturbed for a minimum of 8 hours (24 hours is better for precision).
6. Read the hygrometer **without opening the bag** first.

The result should be 75% RH. If your hygrometer reads 70%, it is running 5 points low, meaning any reading you take inside your humidor will be 5 points lower than reality. Write that offset down. If your device is adjustable, correct it immediately (see the next section). If not, just track the offset mentally every time you check the dial.

## My hygrometer didn't read 75% -- how do I adjust it?

*This one comes up a lot:* the adjustment process differs depending on whether you own an analog or digital model.

**Analog hygrometers** have a small screw on the back, accessible with a standard jeweler's or eyeglass screwdriver. The moment you remove the device from the sealed bag, before the reading has a chance to drift, turn the screw until the needle lands on 75%. That's it. Put it back in the humidor.

**Digital hygrometers** vary by brand. Most have a dedicated offset or calibration button, often labeled "CAL" or accessed by a long-press of the mode button. Consult your manual, but the typical process is: hold the calibration button until the display blinks, then use the up/down arrows to shift the reading to 75%, and confirm. Some budget digital units have no adjustment feature at all. In that case, simply track the offset.

**No adjustment possible?** That's workable. If your hygrometer consistently reads 4 points low, treat every reading as +4. Less convenient, but functionally accurate as long as you apply the correction consistently.

## Analog vs. digital: which type of hygrometer is more accurate for cigars?

*Readers frequently ask:* whether the classic round analog dial or a small digital square is the better call. The honest answer is that digital hygrometers have a meaningful accuracy advantage in most practical situations.

Digital sensors don't rely on springs or coils that physically flex and fatigue. Most quality digital units hold accuracy within 2-3% RH between calibration sessions and give you a simultaneous temperature reading as a bonus. That matters because temperature and relative humidity interact, and because sustained heat above 75 degrees F creates conditions where tobacco beetles can hatch and devastate a collection. That's a risk worth monitoring.

Analog hygrometers remain popular for aesthetic reasons. A polished brass dial looks handsome inside a traditional Spanish cedar humidor, and there's nothing wrong with that preference, as long as you account for the maintenance reality: analog units should be recalibrated at least every 2 months, whereas a quality digital unit needs checking only every 6 months or so. If you own an analog hygrometer and haven't calibrated it since you bought it, there's a reasonable chance it's lying to you right now.

For collectors managing multiple humidors or an aging cabinet with 200+ cigars, Bluetooth-enabled digital hygrometers (like the Boveda Butler or similar devices) log humidity and temperature history and push readings to a smartphone, no opening lids required. When you're tracking how a vitola performs across a multi-year age, that data continuity is genuinely useful. ## How often should I recalibrate, and what triggers an early check?

Scheduled recalibration intervals are a minimum, not a guarantee. Certain events should prompt an immediate check outside your regular schedule:

- **The hygrometer was dropped.** Physical shock can knock the calibration off, especially in analog units.
- **You moved or relocated your humidor.** A new room with different ambient humidity and temperature will stress-test your readings.
- **You notice the lid seal feels looser** or your RH swings seem wider than usual. Your hygrometer may not be the culprit, but it's worth ruling out.
- **You're starting a long-term age.** If you're pulling sticks out of rotation for 12-36 months, verifying your baseline calibration before the age begins protects the whole investment.
- **After seasonal transitions.** Dry winter air or humid summer conditions push humidor environments to their edges, and that's when inaccurate readings do the most damage.

Beyond scheduled checks, developing a feel for your humidor, how it responds to being opened, how quickly it recovers after refilling, gives you a sense of whether the numbers are tracking reality. When something feels off, trust that instinct and run the salt test before assuming it's a humidification problem.

## What happens if I've been storing cigars with an uncalibrated hygrometer?

The consequences depend on which direction the error ran. A hygrometer that has been reading *high* (showing 70% when you're actually at 60%) means your cigars have been too dry. Dry cigars burn hot and fast, lose their aromatic oils, and can crack at the foot or develop runs in the wrapper. The good news: slightly under-humidified cigars can usually be rehabilitated slowly. Bring them back up to target humidity over several weeks rather than shocking them with a fast rehydration, which risks wrapper cracking and uneven moisture distribution.

A hygrometer reading *low* (showing 65% when you're actually at 75-80%) is the more dangerous error. Cigars sitting in excess humidity are at risk for mold, which spreads fast and is difficult to remediate, and for tobacco beetle activity. Beetles thrive in warm, humid conditions and can destroy an entire collection. If you suspect you've been running high, inspect every cigar individually before placing them back in a corrected environment. ## One final thing: where to place the hygrometer inside the humidor

*A question we hear often:* does hygrometer placement actually matter? It does, modestly. Humidity inside a humidor is rarely perfectly uniform. Areas near the humidification source read higher; corners furthest from it read lower. The most representative placement is roughly centered in the humidor, away from both the humidifier and the walls. If your humidor has a tray dividing two levels, the lower level typically holds slightly more moisture than the upper.

For cabinet humidors with multiple zones, using two calibrated hygrometers, one high and one low, gives a clearer picture of how evenly your environment is distributing humidity. If the gap between the two readings consistently exceeds 5%, it's worth examining whether your humidification system is positioned optimally or whether a fan or passive circulation aid would help. Good climate data, like good cigars, only gets better the more precisely you understand it.
