Cigar Nubber: What It Is & How to Use One

By Stick Picks · Published November 20, 2026

Quick Answer

A cigar nubber (also called a cigar dagger, pick, or spike) is a small pointed tool you press into the side of a cigar's last inch or two. It acts as a handle so you can smoke the nub comfortably without burning your fingers, letting you enjoy every last draw of a premium stick.

A cigar nubber is one of those accessories most collectors hear about once and then immediately wish they’d known sooner. If you’ve ever had to put down a $20 robusto two thirds of the way through, not because the flavor was gone but because your fingertips were about to get scorched, you already understand the problem it solves.

Okay, so what exactly is a cigar nubber?

A cigar nubber is a small tool, often resembling a miniature spear or fork, designed to grip the last portion of a cigar, the part commonly called the “nub.” It lets you smoke to the very end without burning your fingers.

The tool goes by a handful of names: cigar dagger, cigar pick, cigar spike, stogie stick. The mechanics are the same regardless of branding. A sharp pointed tool pierces through the last two inches of a cigar, giving you something to hold so the heat stays away from your hand.

While smoking a cigar down to the nub has been around as long as cigars themselves, dedicated nubbers are a relatively modern invention. Historically, smokers would use whatever was on hand, a toothpick, a matchstick, even a paperclip, to hold onto a stub that had gotten too short to grip comfortably. As smoking culture refined itself, specialized tools followed.

Why would I bother? Can’t I just hold the cigar normally?

A question we hear often: yes, you can hold a short cigar with your fingers right up until the moment you can’t. For most smokers that moment arrives around the last inch and a half, well before the flavor actually peaks. The nub, that final section of a hand-rolled premium cigar, is prized because it concentrates everything: oils from the wrapper, binder, and filler have been building with every inch smoked.

Ever notice how a cigar keeps tasting better as you get closer to the end? That concentrated flavor in the last inch is exactly what most smokers abandon in the ashtray, not because they want to, but because heat forces the decision. A nubber removes that constraint.

There’s also a straight economic argument. At the premium tier, cigars in the $20-$35 range, leaving the final 20% unsmoked is real waste. A nubber lets you hold the cigar comfortably at the nub stage and smoke a little longer than you otherwise could.

Most nub tools use a single prong. The two-pronged fork style was invented specifically to fix the spinning problem. If stability matters to you, particularly on larger ring gauges, the fork is worth the small size premium.

Some combo tools also double as a draw improver. Age and storage conditions can tighten even a cigar that drew perfectly when it was fresh. Ribs on the shaft of certain nubbers let you gently push the tool into the cut end and pull it back out, opening a small channel for a smoother draw.

How do I actually use a cigar nubber?

This one comes up a lot: the technique is straightforward, but a few details matter.

Step 1 - Time your entry. Deploy the nubber when your cigar reaches roughly the final inch and a half to two inches. Too early and you’re just adding a handle where you didn’t need one; too late and you’ve already been fighting heat.

Step 2 - Choose your insertion point. Aim for the side of the cigar near the cap (the end you draw from), not near the foot. The goal is to keep your fingers clear of the burning end.

Step 3 - Press firmly but gently. Hand-rolled cigars can be delicate at this stage. Push the spike in at a slight angle, perpendicular to the length of the cigar. You want it to grip the rolled tobacco without splitting the wrapper.

Step 4 - Draw normally. Hold the nubber’s handle, place the cap to your lips, and draw exactly as you would at any other point in the smoke. The burning foot is now safely away from your hand.

Step 5 - Know when to stop. Even with a nubber, there’s an endpoint. Heat travels up eventually and the flavor can turn harsh. Smoke it as long as it’s enjoyable. When it isn’t, put it down.

How is a nubber different from a cigar holder?

A classic cigar holder, think 1920s Hollywood, fits over the head end like a mouthpiece and is used from the moment you light up. It keeps tobacco from reaching your lips and was once considered standard formal smoking etiquette.

A nubber is different in both design and purpose. It’s a side-piercing tool deployed only in the final stretch of a smoke. It doesn’t change how you draw from the cigar, doesn’t add length, and doesn’t sit between your lips. The holder is about keeping the cigar clean from hand to lip throughout the session. The nubber is about gripping a short, hot stub safely right at the end. Two different problems entirely.

Does a nubber work with any size or shape cigar?

It works with the vast majority of formats. Parejos, straight-sided cigars like robustos, toros, and coronas, are the easiest. The cylindrical body gives the spike a uniform surface to grip. Worth noting: age and storage conditions can tighten a draw late in a smoke, so don’t assume the nubber is at fault if resistance increases near the end.

Figurados (torpedoes, belicosos, pyramids) taper toward one or both ends, which makes a single-prong spike less stable as the cigar narrows. A two-prong fork handles those better. Very thin lanceros (38-42 ring gauge) can be fragile at nub stage, so insert with extra care and consider a bamboo disposable to avoid cracking the wrapper.

Can I track which cigars I smoked to the nub?

Genuinely, yes, and it’s more useful than it sounds. If you journal your cigars (a habit that trains your palate over time), noting whether you smoked a particular cigar to the nub is worth logging. If a blend keeps getting nubbed, that’s real information: the flavor holds up all the way through and the blend may be worth buying in quantity. FAQs

What is a cigar nubber?

A cigar nubber is a sharp pointed tool used to pierce through the last two inches of a cigar, giving you something to hold so you can smoke comfortably without burning your fingers.

How do you use a cigar nubber?

When your cigar reaches the final inch and a half, press the nubber’s prong(s) firmly into the side of the cigar near the cap end. Use the handle to draw and hold the cigar while keeping the hot foot away from your hand.

Does using a nubber affect the draw or flavor?

Inserting the spike into the side does not meaningfully alter draw resistance or flavor. Some nubbers double as a draw tool and can gently loosen a tight draw when pressed into the cap end, but the taste of the smoke itself is unaffected.

What’s the difference between a cigar nubber and a cigar holder?

A cigar holder fits over the head end and is used throughout the smoke. A nubber is inserted sideways into the body near the end and is deployed only for the final inch or two of a session.

Are cigar nubbers worth buying?

The nub is arguably the best part of a well-made cigar. If you regularly smoke premium sticks, a nubber pays for itself by letting you actually finish them.

Can a cigar nubber also fix a tight draw?

Some can. A two-in-one tool lets you poke a channel through the cap area to loosen tightly packed tobacco leaves. Not every nubber has this feature, so check the product specs before buying if draw improvement matters to you.
