Why Your Humidor Won't Hold Humidity (and How to Fix It)

By Stick Picks · Published August 1, 2026

Quick Answer

A humidor that won't hold humidity usually fails for one of seven reasons: it was never properly seasoned, the lid seal has a gap, the humidifier is undersized or depleted, the hygrometer is reading wrong, the box is overfilled, it's placed near a heat source, or the wood itself has warped. Fix the root cause and RH stabilizes within a few days.

Humidity trouble is the single most common complaint among new and intermediate collectors, and the frustrating part is that a dying humidor rarely has just one cause. It usually has two or three overlapping problems conspiring at once. This guide walks through every real failure mode, in order of how often they actually appear, with concrete fixes for each.

”I just set up my humidor and the humidity keeps dropping — what did I do wrong?”

A question we hear often: The answer, almost always, is skipped or rushed seasoning. A brand-new humidor’s Spanish cedar interior is kiln-dried during manufacture, meaning the wood is moisture-starved and will aggressively pull humidity out of the air (and your cigars) the moment you close the lid.

Before placing a single stick inside, the wood needs to absorb enough moisture to reach equilibrium. The standard method is placing a small shot glass of distilled water (never tap) inside the closed humidor for 24-48 hours, repeating until the interior RH climbs and stays at your target range without the humidifier working overtime. Boveda’s 84% seasoning packs do the same job passively over 14 days with less risk of over-wetting. Either way, rushing this step means the cedar keeps competing with your humidifier for moisture, and the humidifier loses. For a full walkthrough of the seasoning process, see our guide on how to season a new humidor.

One overlooked nuance: if a humidor has sat empty and dry for months, it needs re-seasoning even if you seasoned it properly the first time. The wood dries back out.

”My humidor is seasoned, but humidity still escapes. How do I find a leak?”

This one comes up a lot: The dollar-bill test is the fastest diagnostic in the hobby. Close a dollar bill in the lid so half hangs out, then pull it. If it slides out with no resistance, your seal is failing somewhere. Repeat around all four edges and corners — leaks are often localized to a single corner where the lid has warped or the hinge has pulled slightly out of alignment.

Even a small gap can let moisture escape faster than you can refill it. Common culprits:

  • Warped lid — wood moves with seasonal humidity. A lid that sealed fine in winter may gap in summer.
  • Worn or missing gasket — some budget humidors use a foam gasket that compresses and flattens over time. Replacing it often solves the problem outright.
  • Loose hinges — hinges that have pulled slightly from the wood create a pivot-point gap at the back of the lid.

A short-term fix for minor warping: place the empty, closed humidor under gentle, even weight (a few hardcover books work fine) for 48 hours in a stable room. For severe warps, a replacement lid or a different storage solution is more practical than fighting the wood.

”What’s wrong with my humidifier — I just refilled it and the RH still won’t climb?”

Readers frequently ask: whether their humidifier is actually the problem or just a symptom. Usually it’s both. Foam and sponge humidifiers are the most common factory inclusions, and they’re also the least reliable over time. If you can’t remember the last time you refilled yours, it’s probably running on fumes.

Beyond depletion, there’s a sizing problem. Manufacturers rate their included humidifiers optimistically — a 50-count humidor’s stock foam unit often can’t keep pace with a well-stocked box in a dry climate. As a rough benchmark, you need approximately 1 square inch of active humidification surface per 10-15 cigars, though passive Boveda packs sidestep this calculation entirely by delivering a precise target RH and stopping automatically. If you’ve upgraded to Boveda or Heartfelt beads and humidity is still dropping, the issue is almost certainly your seal or wood.

One hard rule: cigars stored below 65% RH will start to dry out. Don’t wait for wrappers to crack before investigating.

”Could my hygrometer just be wrong? It says 68% but nothing feels right.”

Yes, and this is the diagnosis that saves collectors the most wasted effort. Analog hygrometers are notoriously inaccurate out of the box and drift steadily over time. A typical analog unit can read ±5-10% off its actual value with no visible indication anything is wrong.

Before tearing apart a perfectly functioning humidor, verify your hygrometer with a salt test: seal a bottle cap containing one teaspoon of table salt moistened with a few drops of distilled water in a zip-lock bag with the hygrometer for 6 hours. At equilibrium, it should read 75% ± 1%. Any variance is your offset — factor it into every future reading. Digital units with a calibration button (like the Inkbird or Govee Bluetooth sensors) make this adjustment electronically in seconds, which is why most serious collectors have moved away from analog entirely.

Location matters more than most people expect. Placing a desktop humidor on a windowsill, above a refrigerator, or near a heating vent are three of the most common mistakes — all create localized temperature spikes that drive humidity out through the lid faster than any humidifier can compensate.

”I’ve fixed everything — how do I know when my humidor has actually stabilized?”

Give it time and stop opening the lid. Every time you open a humidor you release a puff of conditioned air and admit ambient air. If you’re checking every few hours, you’re creating the instability you’re trying to measure. Check once every 24 hours maximum during the recovery period.

A well-seasoned desktop humidor with a quality passive humidifier (Boveda or beads) should need meaningful attention only once every 4-6 weeks: a refill or pack swap, a quick hygrometer check, and nothing more. That kind of stability is earned by establishing a good baseline and then leaving the system alone.

For collectors managing larger inventories (say, over 150 sticks), the physics change. The practical inflection point is around 200 cigars, particularly for anyone aging sticks over a year rather than cycling through stock monthly. At that scale, a single desktop box can’t reliably buffer seasonal swings. Moving to a coolidor, tupperdor, or wineador gives you proportionally more wood mass and a better seal-to-volume ratio, both of which make stable RH much easier to maintain.

Tracking your humidor’s RH over time, not just spot-checking it, is where Stick Picks can help. Logging your readings alongside your cigar notes gives you a historical baseline, so you can spot a slow humidity drift weeks before it damages your collection instead of discovering it when a wrapper cracks.

”Should I be worried if humidity runs a few points high instead of low?”

Readers frequently ask: whether high humidity (above 72-74%) is a serious risk. It is. Low humidity dries and cracks cigars, but high humidity encourages mold growth and can trigger tobacco beetle hatching if temperatures climb above 70°F (21°C) at the same time. That combination of high RH and warm temperature is exactly what sends a collection into an infestation. If you’re running consistently high, switch to a lower-rated Boveda pack (65% instead of 69%), improve ventilation, and check our guide on cigar beetles if you spot any bore holes.

For most collectors in stable indoor environments, the sweet spot sits between 65-70% RH at 65-70°F — slightly drier than the traditional “70/70” rule that dominated cigar culture for decades, and favored by most modern blenders for long-term storage.

FAQs

How long does it take for a humidor to stabilize after fixing a humidity problem? Typically 48-72 hours after addressing the root cause: re-seasoning, refilling the humidifier, or resealing. Larger cabinets may take 5-7 days to fully equalize.

Can I use tap water instead of distilled water to refill my humidifier? No. Tap water contains minerals that clog foam and gel humidifiers, breed bacteria, and can deposit residue on your cigars. Always use distilled water or the manufacturer’s propylene glycol solution.

My hygrometer reads 75% but my cigars feel dry — what’s happening? Your hygrometer is almost certainly out of calibration. Analog units drift by ±5-10% with no warning. Run a salt test or use a Boveda One-Step Calibration Kit to verify before trusting any reading.

Will adding more cigars cause humidity to drop? Yes, temporarily. New cigars are dry and absorb moisture from the box as they acclimate. Stagger additions and add extra humidification capacity when restocking; humidity should recover within a week.

Is it normal for humidity to swing 3-5% during the day? Minor swings of 2-4% RH are normal, especially in desktop humidors in rooms with fluctuating temperatures. If swings exceed 5-6% daily, investigate your seal, humidifier size, or room placement.
