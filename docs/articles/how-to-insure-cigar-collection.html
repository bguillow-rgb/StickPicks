How to Insure Your Cigar Collection: A Collector's Guide

By Stick Picks · Published October 4, 2026

Quick Answer

Most standard homeowners policies cover only $500, $2,000 of personal property collectibles, far less than a serious humidor is worth. To fully protect your cigar collection, you need a documented inventory, purchase receipts, and either a personal articles floater or a specialized collectibles policy added to your existing coverage.

Buying a box of Padrón 1964s, a few aged Cubans, and a cabinet humidor adds up faster than most collectors expect. When your humidor holds $2,000, $5,000, or more in tobacco, the question stops being if you need insurance and starts being what kind. Most collectors never think about this until something goes wrong.

Does my regular homeowners policy already cover my cigars?

A question we hear often: yes, but probably not nearly enough.

Your cigar collection is generally covered against fire, wind, and other perils named in your homeowners policy, where it falls under the personal property section. That sounds reassuring. The catch is the dollar cap. Most homeowners policies cover only $500 to $2,000 worth of collectibles, which means a high-value collection is almost certainly underinsured from day one.

There’s a secondary hit for smoking collectors: unless you don’t actually smoke the cigars, you may lose the nonsmokers discount available in some states, which can save up to 20% on your premium. That applies to any household member who smokes tobacco products.

The practical upshot: run a quick mental tally of what’s in your humidor right now. If the retail replacement cost exceeds $1,500-$2,000, your standard policy is likely leaving a gap. That’s the threshold at which it’s worth a conversation with your agent, not after a house fire.

So what’s the right type of coverage for a serious collection?

To make sure your collectibles are covered in the event of a loss, you’ll want to purchase scheduled personal coverage, also known as collectors insurance. It’s a supplemental form of personal property insurance that covers valuables exceeding or not covered by your standard homeowners policy.

There are two practical paths most collectors take.

The first is a personal articles floater (PAF) or rider added to your existing homeowners or renters policy. Depending on the size of your collection, you’ll likely need a rider, which usually requires an appraisal or receipts to prove value. This is the most common and affordable route for mid-tier collections.

The second is a standalone collectibles policy issued by a specialized insurer. Specialized insurance carriers, the most well-known being Lloyd’s of London, provide all-risk policies on the specific items you choose to insure. For very large or rare collections, particularly those containing aged pre-embargo Cubans or verified limited releases, this route provides the broadest protection.

Specialized coverage can start at around $75 a year for $10,000 worth of blanket coverage that protects every item at full value. For collectors whose humidors have grown quietly over years of box purchases, that’s often a surprisingly affordable outcome.

What’s the difference between agreed value and stated value, and why does it matter for cigars?

This one comes up a lot: the wording in your policy determines exactly how much you receive if your collection is destroyed.

Consider opting for “agreed value” coverage. Most specialized insurance is sold on agreed value, which is the value that you and your insurer mutually agree to in advance, and you’ll need photos and receipts to help get an accurate quote.

With agreed value coverage, the amount paid if damage or loss occurs is the previously agreed-upon amount set by both you and the insurer. No depreciation deducted, no negotiation after the fact. The alternative, stated value, lets the insurer still apply depreciation to your payout. For tobacco, which most carriers treat as a consumable, this distinction matters enormously. Always confirm which basis your policy uses before signing.

How do I actually document my collection for an insurance claim?

Readers frequently ask: what does “documentation” really mean in practice?

If those special items do have value, it’s important to document their condition, record the contents, and provide adequate protection. Without a detailed review, you may over- or underestimate the value of your collectibles and not know how to properly insure them.

Here’s what to record for every box or significant single:

Item to documentWhy it matters
Brand, line, and vitolaEstablishes identity for replacement quotes
Box date code / production dateConfirms provenance and age premium
Purchase date and retailerProvides a paper trail for original cost
Purchase price (receipt preferred)The baseline for replacement value
Current market valueParticularly important for aged or discontinued lines
Photographs (box, bands, condition)Visual proof of condition at time of documentation

Getting coverage will require providing the insurance company with receipts, provenance, and proof that your items are worth the value claimed. The more complete your records, the smoother any claim will be.

Do I need a professional appraisal?

For everyday working collections, a mix of current-production premiums in the $10-$25 range, purchase receipts and a thorough photo inventory are generally enough to satisfy an insurer. A professional appraiser can be useful because their documentation helps establish a value, which in turn lets you get the right level of coverage. For insurance purposes, you’ll want to know the “retail replacement value,” the highest amount required to replace an item with a similar piece.

A formal appraisal makes more sense when your humidor holds:

  • Aged pre-embargo Cuban cigars, where secondary market prices diverge significantly from original retail
  • Discontinued limited editions no longer available at standard retail
  • Cellar-aged stock with a value premium above current MSRP
  • Collections exceeding $10,000 in total value, where the appraisal cost is trivial relative to the coverage gap

If you decide later to sell a portion or all of your collection, the appraisal can also help establish a market value. It serves double duty: protection now, documentation for a potential future sale.

What about Cuban cigars, can they be insured in the U.S.?

This is one of the genuinely tricky corners of cigar insurance. Standard all-risk policies may not cover Cuban cigars, as you would need to prove they are for collection purposes, are non-consumable, and have a certified stated value established up front, and even then the carrier has the final word.

Pre-2016 Cuban cigars legally acquired abroad and brought through customs within the personal exemption limit are the cleanest situation. Cigars acquired through gray-market channels present obvious documentation problems. If your collection includes significant Cuban inventory, be upfront with your insurance agent. The worst outcome is discovering a coverage exclusion after a loss.

What’s a realistic premium for cigar collection insurance?

Cost varies by carrier, collection value, storage conditions, and your existing homeowners policy, but the general landscape looks like this:

Coverage amountApproximate annual costBest for
Up to $5,000 (PAF rider)$25-$75/yearGrowing mid-tier collection
$5,000-$25,000 (scheduled floater)$75-$250/yearSerious collector with box inventory
$25,000+ (specialized policy)$250-$600+/yearLarge cellar, rare Cubans, limited editions

These are rough industry benchmarks. Your actual quote depends on your carrier and deductible choice. Some insurers may also set a minimum value level for the entire collection before it qualifies for coverage. If your collection is still small, a rider to your existing policy is almost always the right first move.

The fastest way to get ready right now

Insurance conversations go faster when you walk in prepared. Before you call your agent:

  1. Run a value estimate. Total up what you paid for everything in your humidor. Use current retail prices for items without receipts.
  2. Photograph everything. Every box label, every band on significant singles, and the interior of your humidor with the lid open.
  3. Pull your receipts. Online retailers email receipts, so search your inbox for cigar retailer names and export to a folder.
  4. Note special items. Aged stock, limited editions, and anything with provenance beyond standard retail deserves a line item.
  5. Ask your agent two specific questions: (a) What is my current personal property cap for collectibles? (b) What documentation do you need to add a PAF?

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover my cigar collection?

Partially. Standard homeowners and renters policies cover personal property against fire, theft, and named perils, but they cap collectible payouts, often at $500 to $2,000. A serious humidor with premium or limited-edition cigars can easily exceed that cap, leaving you underinsured without a separate rider.

What is a personal articles floater (PAF) for cigars?

A personal articles floater is a supplemental policy or rider added to your homeowners or renters policy that extends coverage to specific high-value items. It requires documentation, receipts, photos, and sometimes a formal appraisal, and covers the agreed replacement value rather than depreciated value.

Do I need an appraisal to insure my cigar collection?

Not always, but it helps. For collections under a few thousand dollars, purchase receipts and a detailed photo inventory often suffice. For larger or rare collections, especially those containing aged Cuban cigars or limited releases, insurers may require a certified appraisal to establish replacement value.

What's the difference between agreed value and stated value for cigar insurance?

Agreed value means if your collection is lost or destroyed, you receive the full amount you and the insurer agreed upon upfront, no depreciation. Stated value means the insurer may pay up to that amount but can still apply depreciation. For cigars, always push for agreed value coverage.

What should I document to insure my cigar collection?

Photograph every box and single, record brand, vitola, quantity, purchase date, purchase price, and current estimated market value. Keep digital receipts from retailers. Store documentation in cloud backup outside your home. A running inventory app makes this dramatically easier to maintain and produce on demand.
