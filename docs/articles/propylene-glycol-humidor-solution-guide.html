Propylene Glycol Humidor Solution: Complete How-To Guide

By Stick Picks · Published November 2, 2026

Quick Answer

Propylene glycol (PG) humidor solution is a pre-mixed or DIY 50/50 blend of PG and distilled water. Pour it into a passive foam or crystal humidifier element, never directly on cigars or cedar. It self-regulates near 70% RH by absorbing excess moisture and releasing it when the air dries out.

Propylene glycol humidor solution, sometimes called “PG solution,” “50/50 solution,” or just “humidor solution” on retailer shelves, has been around for decades, yet it remains one of the most misunderstood tools in a collector’s kit. Plenty of enthusiasts have a bottle sitting next to their humidor and aren’t fully sure when to use it, what it actually does, or whether it’s even still the right choice given all the modern alternatives. This guide covers every angle.

What exactly is propylene glycol, and why does anyone put it in a humidor?

Propylene glycol is a synthetic chemical with hygroscopic properties, meaning it can both absorb and retain moisture from surrounding air. That two-way behavior is precisely what makes it useful in a humidor. Unlike plain distilled water, which keeps releasing humidity until the element runs dry, propylene glycol stops releasing moisture once roughly 70% RH is reached, and it absorbs excess moisture from the air when humidity climbs above that threshold.

The practical result is a built-in buffer. When ambient conditions are dry, the solution releases water vapor. When conditions are humid, it mops up the excess. That self-regulating loop is the core value of PG over distilled water alone, and it’s why tobacconists have relied on it since long before Boveda packs entered the picture.

In a cigar humidor solution, propylene glycol and distilled water work in tandem to provide ideal relative humidity and also inhibit mold and bacteria growth, a secondary benefit that distilled water alone can’t offer.

How do I actually mix and use it? What’s the right ratio?

A question we hear often: collectors buy a bottle of “humidor solution” without reading whether it’s pre-mixed or concentrate, and then either use it wrong or panic.

The standard formulation is a 50/50 blend by volume of propylene glycol and distilled water. Most commercial products, Xikar, Famous Smoke, Joe Cool, Victory Cigars, arrive pre-mixed at this ratio, so you can use them straight from the bottle. Pure propylene glycol on its own is not recommended; it needs to be mixed with distilled water to provide the correct relative humidity for storing and aging cigars.

Here’s the step-by-step for a standard foam or floral-foam humidifier element:

  1. Hold the element over a sink.
  2. Squirt the propylene glycol solution directly into the vents until the foam is saturated.
  3. Wait for the excess water to drain before placing the unit back in your humidor, or it will leak a puddle directly onto your cigars.
  4. Return the element to its mount and close the lid.

For crystal gel jars, the approach is similar: fill to the marked line with PG solution and let the crystals absorb it fully before placing inside. Many collectors use distilled water for regular refills and add propylene glycol solution to the humidification unit every 3-4 cycles rather than every single refill, which is a reasonable middle-ground.

One detail worth stressing: don’t try to mix a DIY humidor solution at home unless you have a precise scale or graduated cylinders. Even a modest deviation from the 50/50 ratio shifts the equilibrium RH point noticeably. Off-the-shelf solutions are inexpensive and reliably formulated. The variability simply isn’t worth it.

Does a 50/50 PG solution really hold exactly 70% RH?

Readers frequently ask: “The label says 70%, but my hygrometer reads 73%. What’s wrong?”

Nothing, necessarily. A 50/50 PG-to-water solution equilibrates to approximately 70% RH under standard conditions, but ambient temperature shifts the actual output. At 60 degrees F, maintaining 65% RH would require a 70/30 PG-to-water ratio by weight. At warmer room temperatures the 50/50 blend typically reads closer to 68-72%, which aligns well with the traditional “70/70 rule” most collectors target.

When the humidity level in your humidor drops below 70%, the propylene glycol forces the distilled water into the air until 70% humidity is achieved. When it rises above 70%, the propylene glycol attracts moisture from the air and stores it in your humidification unit. That self-correcting mechanism works passively with no batteries, no electronics, and no subscription.

That said, most humidifiers that use PG tend to over-humidify and lack regulatory properties to reduce excess RH during spikes from ambient humidity or from cigars that arrived over-hydrated from the store. This is why pairing a PG element with a calibrated analog or digital hygrometer, and logging your readings weekly, is standard practice for any serious collector. For collectors who prefer a set-it-and-check-it-monthly approach to a modest desktop humidor, PG solution in a foam or gel element hits a sweet spot of affordability and reliability. For those aging high-value sticks long-term, where dialing in 62%, 65%, or 69% precisely matters, Boveda packs are generally the stronger choice since each pack is factory-calibrated to a specific RH level.

According to cigar industry retail data, Boveda has become the dominant humidification method in premium humidors in North America since roughly 2018, but PG solution retains a strong user base globally, particularly in markets where humidity packs aren’t as readily available.

How often do I need to recharge or refill the PG solution?

A question we hear often: “I filled the element two weeks ago and my RH already dropped. Did I do something wrong?”

Refill frequency depends on four variables: humidor size, seal quality, how often you open the lid, and ambient relative humidity in your home. A well-sealed 50-count desktop humidor in a humidity-stable room might need a top-up every three to four weeks. A larger, older humidor with a worn seal, or one in a very dry climate, might need attention every ten to fourteen days.

The reliable signal is your hygrometer. When your reading drops more than 3-4 points below your target RH on two consecutive checks, it’s time to recharge the element. Regular maintenance helps preserve the effectiveness of the PG solution; always use distilled water in any top-up to keep your humidor solution working properly. If you’re topping up with distilled water rather than doing a full recharge of PG solution, that’s fine for day-to-day maintenance. The PG compound itself doesn’t evaporate. It’s the water fraction that depletes. Replacing the entire solution with fresh PG mixture every six months or so flushes out accumulated impurities and keeps the element performing at its best.

Can I season a new humidor with propylene glycol solution?

Short answer: it’s a useful supplement, not a primary seasoning method. Propylene glycol is generally recommended over distilled water for the preparation process, as its viscosity helps prevent over-humidifying the humidor. The actual seasoning process, saturating the Spanish cedar walls so they stop drawing moisture from your cigars, still requires slowly introducing humidity to the wood over one to three days using a damp sponge, a shot glass of distilled water, or purpose-built seasoning packs. Filling the humidifier element with PG solution during or after that process is a smart step that sets you up for stable ongoing maintenance. FAQs

Can I use pure propylene glycol in my humidor without mixing it with water? No. Pure propylene glycol pulls moisture out of the air rather than releasing it, which can actually dry your cigars. Always use a 50/50 mixture with distilled water, or buy a pre-mixed commercial solution.

Will propylene glycol solution damage silica bead humidifiers? Yes. Silica beads are pre-treated with PG during manufacturing and should only be recharged with distilled water. Adding more PG solution can disrupt their calibrated RH output and shorten their lifespan.

Is propylene glycol safe for cigars and the tobacco inside? Yes. Propylene glycol is a safe solution for prepping your humidor and humidifying your cigars. It is food-grade and widely used in food, pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics. At normal humidor concentrations it poses no risk to the tobacco or to the person smoking the cigar.

Can I use tap water instead of distilled water to mix my PG solution? Do not use tap water in your humidor. Tap water contains microscopic bacteria which can lead to mold and ruin your cigars and your humidor. Always use distilled water, available at any grocery store for roughly $1 per gallon.

How often should I recharge my humidor’s foam element with PG solution? Check your hygrometer weekly. Most foam elements need a top-up every 2-4 weeks depending on humidor size, how often you open it, and ambient humidity. When RH drops more than 3-4 points below target on consecutive readings, it’s time to recharge.
